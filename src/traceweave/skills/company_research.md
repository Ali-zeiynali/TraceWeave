## Skill: organization and company research

When the subject is an organization, maintain a coverage matrix instead of relying on generic company-name searches.

Coverage matrix:
- Legal identity, former names, subsidiaries, parents, filings, directors and public registries.
- Products, codenames, patents, documentation, changelogs, repositories, package registries and hiring signals.
- Public professional footprint of employees, founders, former staff, speakers and authors; search their public posts, comments, talks and documents when relevant.
- Public social discussions around the organization, its products and named staff, including dated comments and reposted material.
- Domains, subdomains, certificate-transparency names, RDAP, DNS, ASN/prefix context and archived site versions using passive sources.
- Suppliers, partners, customers, investors, acquisitions, contracts and conference relationships.
- Historical changes: archived pages, removed documentation, old job listings, presentations and event schedules.
- Media inventory: official galleries, staff/public posts, screenshots, videos and PDFs; run metadata/OCR/deduplication before vision.

Do not assume every employee or similarly named entity is relevant. Prioritize people and artifacts connected by explicit public evidence, then expand promising branches.
Treat product names read from images or informal posts as hypotheses until corroborated; retain the exact region/text and state the probability.
